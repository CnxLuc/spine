Spine

Privacy policy

Last updated 7 October 2026, for Claude Code and Codex

Spine collects no data for its developer. It has no servers, no analytics and no tracking. When you agree to have articles read, Spine sends the text of the articles you open to the reader you chose: your own Claude Code or Codex, or Anthropic’s API with your key.

What Spine sends, and where

When you open Spine on a page, and you have agreed to have articles read, Spine sends:

  • the article’s title, author, site name and language
  • the article’s text, split into numbered sentences, with its headings, list items and image captions

It sends them to one of these, whichever you chose in Spine’s settings:

  • Claude Code on your computer, which sends them to Anthropic under your Claude plan and Anthropic’s consumer terms and privacy policy
  • Codex on your computer, which sends them to OpenAI under your ChatGPT plan and OpenAI’s terms of use and privacy policy
  • Anthropic’s API at api.anthropic.com, with your API key, under Anthropic’s commercial terms and privacy policy

To reach Claude Code or Codex, Spine uses a small connector program you install yourself. Your browser starts it only when Spine asks for a reading; it hands the article to your own Claude Code or Codex, returns the reply and exits. It keeps nothing, and it never reads your Claude or ChatGPT sign-in.

With the API, Spine sends the article straight from your browser to Anthropic, with your key. It does not pass through any server run by Spine’s developer.

Spine sends each article once. It saves the notes in your browser, so opening the article again sends nothing.

If you change Spine’s API address in its settings, Spine sends API requests to that address instead.

What Spine keeps in your browser

  • your Anthropic API key, in Spine’s extension storage on this device
  • the notes Claude wrote for the last 400 articles you read, and your place in each
  • which reader you chose, whether you agreed to have articles read, and when
  • your settings, such as theme and text size, which your browser may sync to your other devices if you use its sync

None of this leaves your browser, apart from your settings through your browser’s sync and your key sent to Anthropic as described above.

Other things Spine does when you ask

  • Images and site icons load from the article’s own website, as they would if you read the page normally.
  • The copy actions put text on your clipboard.
  • Save to Obsidian passes a note to the Obsidian app on your device.

What Spine never does

  • sell or share your data
  • use your data for advertising, analytics or to decide creditworthiness
  • read pages you have not opened Spine on

Spine’s use of the information it handles follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Stop or delete

  • To stop sending articles, turn off “Send the articles I open to be read” in Spine’s settings.
  • To remove the connector for Claude Code and Codex, run its installer again with --uninstall.
  • To delete saved notes and reading places, select Clear saved notes in Spine’s settings.
  • To delete everything Spine keeps, remove Spine from your browser.

Anthropic and OpenAI keep what they receive according to their own policies.

Changes to this policy

If Spine ever changes what it sends or where, it will tell you in the extension and ask again before sending anything.
